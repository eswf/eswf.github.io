## Synopsis

A slow-moving frontal system carrying high precipitable water (PWAT) is moving across Central and Eastern Europe. The primary weather hazard is embedded convection and training thunderstorms within a broad stratiform rain shield. High rain rates from repeated thunderstorm cores passing over the same catchments present a significant flash flood risk.

## Moderate (MDT) Risk: Northeastern Poland, Kaliningrad Oblast, Lithuania, and Western Latvia

Convective Mode & Evolution: Widespread rain with embedded, intense thunderstorm cores. Training convective lines are expected to repeatedly track along the same axis overnight and into tomorrow morning.

Peak Rain Rates: Thunderstorm cores will easily produce 20 to 40 mm/h.

Storm Accumulations: Total rainfall swaths of 60–110 mm where embedded cores repeatedly overlap.

Threats: High probability of rapid-onset flash flooding. Small rivers, creeks, and urban drainage networks in low-lying areas face severe inundation risk.

## Slight (SLGT) Risk: Central to Northern Poland and Eastern Baltics

Convective Mode & Evolution: Broad rain area containing embedded convective cells and occasional elevated thunderstorms.

Peak Rain Rates: Hourly precipitation rates generally between 10 and 20 mm/h.

Storm Accumulations: Accumulations ranging from 25 to 50 mm.

Threats: Localized flash flooding and street ponding, mainly in urban areas or catchments with poor drainage capacities.

## Marginal (MRGL) Risk: Western & Southern Poland Margins

Convective Mode & Evolution: Predominantly stratiform rainfall with weak, disorganized embedded showers.

Peak Rain Rates: Short bursts under 10 mm/h.

Threats: Low flash flood potential. Minor water pooling on roads and nuisance runoff.
