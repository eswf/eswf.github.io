## HIGH RISK OUTLOOK: SOUTHERN FRANCE / GULF OF LION

A rare, high-end convective setup is expected to materialize over southern France and the immediate coastal waters of the Gulf of Lion during the afternoon and evening hours.

Extremely rich low-level moisture interacting with robust synoptic forcing will yield an exceptionally unstable airmass, with ML-CAPE surging into the 2000–2400 J/kg range. This high thermodynamic instability will overlap with intense vertical wind shear and extreme lower-level directional helicity near the coastal baroclinic zone.

Composite indices depict a top-tier severe environment, featuring Supercell Composite Parameter (SCP) values exceeding 25–30 and a Significant Tornado Parameter (STP) peaking between 5.0–9.0+ across the Languedoc-Roussillon coastline towards the Rhone Delta.

## Primary Hazards:

Tornadoes: A HIGH RISK has been designated primarily due to the threat of strong, potentially long-track tornadoes (IF2+). Lowering LCL heights over the immediate coastal interface combined with intense 0–1 km SRH will heavily favor tornadic mesocyclones.

Giant Hail: Very large to giant hail (5-7cm) is highly likely with any discrete supercell given the high CAPE density in the hail growth zone and extreme updraft strength.

Severe Wind: Destructive convective wind gusts (100-120km/h) are anticipated, particularly if convection transitions into bowing segments or high-precipitation (HP) supercell clusters.
